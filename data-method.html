Data & Method

Everything on this site comes from one public NASA service, is cached as plain JSON, and is processed with two standard statistical tests. This page shows the whole chain: where the numbers come from, how a verdict is reached, what the numbers cannot tell you, and how AI was used.

Method

How a verdict is reached

01 · m × 12

Remove the seasonal cycle

Every value has its own long-term average for that calendar month subtracted. What is left is the anomaly: how unusual that month was for its time of year. This is why a wet January is not counted as evidence of a wet climate.

02 · Theil–Sen

Estimate the rate of change

Take the slope of every possible pair of points in the record and use the median. Unlike a normal line fit, one broken sensor or one huge storm cannot drag the answer. The 95% interval is the band drawn around the trend line.

03 · Mann–Kendall

Test whether the trend is real

A rank test that does not assume the data are normally distributed. It counts how often later values exceed earlier ones and turns that count into S, Kendall's tau and a p-value.

04 · p < 0.05

Make the call

If the p-value is below 0.05 we report a significant increase or decrease. If not, the verdict is no significant trend — never "no trend", because a shorter or noisier record simply does not carry enough evidence yet.

Rate shown on screen

per decade = slope × 12 × 10 (monthly)  ·  slope × 365.25 × 10 (daily)

Percent change

mean(last 3 years) − mean(first 3 years) ÷ |mean(first 3 years)|

Reproduction check against the published record

Our cache reproduces well-known features of the observed record rather than inventing them. Two examples from the six regions, both visible in Investigate:

  • Sahel rainfall recovery. Precipitation over the western Sahel rises by roughly 0.2 mm/day per decade in this record, consistent with the documented post-1980s recovery after the great droughts.
  • Nile Delta warming. Air temperature rises by about 0.3 °C per decade, above the global land average for the same period — the regional amplification expected over a desert-adjacent basin.
  • Amazon drying signal. Precipitation and evapotranspiration both decline, which is the direction reported in the literature on Amazon forest–atmosphere coupling.

These are consistency checks, not independent validation: a reanalysis is a model constrained by observations, so agreement with published estimates partly reflects shared input data.

Honest limits

What this tool cannot do

Linear only

A single straight trend cannot describe a step change, a tipping point or a cycle that turns around. We report one slope because it is the honest baseline, not because the world is linear.

Correlation is not cause

The case files and the Compare page always say associated with. Two series can share a driver, or one can lead the other, and this app cannot tell those apart.

Reanalysis, not a rain gauge

MERRA-2 is a model fed by observations. In data-sparse regions it is closer to an informed estimate than to a measurement, and soil moisture in particular is modelled, not sensed directly.

Six boxes, eight variables

This is a curated cache, not the whole Earth. A region box hides strong local gradients — a delta is not uniform, and an average over it is a simplification.

Serial correlation

Monthly series are autocorrelated, which can make the Mann–Kendall test slightly too optimistic. We show tau and the CI so the strength of the evidence stays visible.

Missing values are reported

Gaps are counted, never filled. Above 20% missing, the case itself tells you to be careful.

AI-use disclosure

How AI was used in this project

Code

AI assistance was used to write and review the front-end code (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), the Python data-fetch and cache script, and the statistical routines. The team reviewed the logic and tested the outputs against synthetic series with known answers (see the self-test above).

Text

AI assistance was used to draft and edit the plain-language explanations and the case narratives. Every numeric claim displayed in the app is computed from the cached NASA series at run time; the prose is reviewed by the team.

In-app assistant

“Ask Trendoscope” does not call a language model in this build. It is a deterministic answer engine that assembles sentences only from the current result object (slope, p-value, tau, mean, missing share) and a fixed glossary, and it declines questions outside the project's domain. The architecture is ready to swap in a grounded LLM behind a server-side proxy, where the API key never reaches the browser.

Design

The interface was designed with AI assistance against the team's own specification documents; all data values, region choices and scientific framing were decided by the team.

No AI system generated any of the data. Values come from NASA POWER; the cache date is recorded in data-core.js.
